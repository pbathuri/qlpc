\begin{abstract}
We introduce \emph{language-conditioned} QAOA: a pipeline in which a large language model (LLM) reads
financial news and emits per-asset convictions that tilt the cost Hamiltonian of a cardinality-constrained
Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) portfolio optimizer, letting natural language steer a
quantum optimizer. We then ask, with strong bias controls, whether the language adds out-of-sample value.
It appears to: a 2024-released LLM (qwen2.5-14B), whose training overlaps the evaluation window, beats
matched random tilts by $+0.76\%$/quarter ($p\!\approx\!0$). But a \emph{genuinely out-of-sample} LLM
(Llama~2, cutoff Sept.\ 2022) on the \emph{same} headlines yields $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, indistinguishable
from random): the benefit appears only when the model's training overlaps the test period---consistent
with \emph{training-horizon hindsight} rather than prediction (the pre-cutoff model is also smaller, a
caveat we note). Our contributions are the language$\to$quantum conditioning pipeline, a reusable
bias-control template, and a quantified warning about a likely training-horizon look-ahead in
LLM-for-finance evaluation.
\keywords{QAOA \and Quantum optimization \and LLM-conditioning \and Look-ahead bias \and Quantum finance.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Pipeline and Method}
Two recent lines sit either side of a gap: Lee et al.~\cite{lee2025llmbl} drive Black--Litterman views
with an LLM but optimize \emph{classically}, while Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023qbl} bring numerically
predicted views into a \emph{quantum} pipeline with no natural language. We occupy the middle---an LLM's
language directly conditions the quantum optimizer---and treat ``do LLM news views help?'' as a
\emph{measurement problem}. Holding $B$ of $n$ assets to maximize the mean--variance objective
$\mu^\top x - q\,x^\top\Sigma x$ over an objective universe (Dow~30, CRSP total returns, shrinkage
covariance), we map the penalized binary problem to a diagonal Ising Hamiltonian solved by
QAOA~\cite{farhi2014qaoa}. A local LLM reads RavenPack news headlines time-stamped \emph{strictly before}
each quarterly decision date into convictions $v_i\in[-1,1]$; a $\pm10\%$ view tilts $\mu\to\mu+s\,v$ (the
sentiment-to-view convention~\cite{black1992litterman}). Estimation uses the train window only; evaluation
the held-out next quarter. Bias controls: hundreds of (subset $\times$ window) instances from a fixed
seed, pre-registered metrics, a matched-random-tilt \emph{placebo}, an off-the-shelf-sentiment (RavenPack
ESS) ablation, and---decisively---two LLMs on the \emph{same} headlines: \textbf{qwen2.5-14B} (released
2024, training \emph{overlaps} the window) and \textbf{Llama~2} (cutoff Sept.\ 2022, \emph{before} it).
The language effect is measured at the exact optimum to separate it from optimizer noise.

\section{Results}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.74\textwidth]{compare_clean_vs_contaminated.pdf}
\caption{The out-of-sample premium of LLM-conditioned tilts over matched random tilts collapses from
$+0.76\%$ ($p\!\approx\!0$) with a training-overlapping LLM to $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$) with a pre-cutoff LLM
on the same headlines (left); per-quarter effects over four decision dates (right).}
\label{fig:main}
\end{figure}
Over 1{,}600 instances (Dow~30, $n{=}14$ qubits, hold~5, four quarterly dates 2023-12--2024-09), the
training-overlapping LLM beats matched random tilts by a language-specific premium of $+0.76\%$/quarter
($p\!\approx\!0$, positive in 3/4 quarters); the pre-cutoff LLM, on identical headlines, yields $-0.23\%$
($p=0.94$, 2/4)---indistinguishable from random (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}). The benefit appears only with the
training-overlapping model. The conditioning has real mechanism leverage (a $\pm10\%$ view changes the
exact optimum in $47$--$68\%$ of instances), yet QAOA at depth $p{=}4$ recovers the true optimum only
$7.7\%$ of the time; we make no quantum-advantage claim and run the economic test at the exact optimum
because the solver is too noisy. Limitations: four decision dates (instance-level $p$ is
pseudo-replicated); the clean and contaminated LLMs differ in capability as well as training cutoff, so
the two are not fully separated; small-$n$ exact simulation; large-cap US; no transaction costs. None
overturn the robust finding---the apparent benefit is absent for a pre-cutoff model. The takeaway is a
transparent language$\to$quantum pipeline and a quantified warning: an LLM whose training overlaps the
test period can show out-of-sample ``alpha'' that a pre-cutoff model does not, a likely training-horizon
look-ahead that LLM-for-finance studies should guard against by dating their model against their
backtest. All results use fixed seeds and deterministic data caches; code will be released upon
publication.
