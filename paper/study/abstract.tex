\title{Does Language Really Help a Quantum Portfolio Optimizer? A Training-Horizon Confound in
LLM-Conditioned QAOA}
\titlerunning{A Training-Horizon Confound in LLM-Conditioned QAOA}
\author{Pradyot Bathuri\inst{1}\orcidID{0009-0002-7893-3006}}

\institute{Indiana University, Bloomington, USA \\ \email{pbathuri@iu.edu}}

\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly turn financial text into views that drive allocation, and
quantum optimizers are proposed for the discrete selection these require. We build a bias-controlled
pipeline bridging the two---an LLM reads point-in-time news into per-asset convictions that tilt the cost
Hamiltonian of a cardinality-constrained Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) portfolio
optimizer---and ask whether the language adds out-of-sample value. It appears to: an LLM whose training
overlaps the evaluation window beats matched random tilts by $+0.76\%$/quarter ($p\!\approx\!0$). But a
\emph{genuinely out-of-sample} LLM (Llama~2, cutoff Sept.\ 2022) on the \emph{same} headlines yields
$-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, indistinguishable from random): the apparent signal is the model's
\emph{training-horizon hindsight}, not prediction. We contribute the methodology and a concrete warning
about a subtle, pervasive confound in LLM-for-finance backtests.
\keywords{QAOA \and Quantum optimization \and LLM-conditioning \and Look-ahead bias \and Quantum finance.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and Method}
Two recent lines sit either side of a gap: Lee et al.~\cite{lee2025llmbl} drive Black--Litterman views
with an LLM but optimize \emph{classically}, while Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023qbl} bring numerically
predicted views into a \emph{quantum} pipeline with no language. We occupy the middle and treat ``do LLM
news views help?'' as a \emph{measurement problem}. The universe is an index (Dow~30), not author-chosen;
returns are CRSP total returns with shrinkage covariance. Holding $B$ of $n$ assets to maximize the
mean--variance objective $\mu^\top x - q\,x^\top\Sigma x$, we map the penalized binary problem to a
diagonal Ising Hamiltonian solved by QAOA~\cite{farhi2014qaoa}. An LLM reads RavenPack headlines
time-stamped \emph{strictly before} each quarterly decision date into convictions $v_i\in[-1,1]$; a
$\pm10\%$ view tilts $\mu\to\mu+s\,v$ (the sentiment-to-view convention~\cite{black1992litterman}).
Estimation uses the train window only; evaluation the held-out next quarter. Bias controls: hundreds of
(subset $\times$ window) instances from a fixed seed, pre-registered metrics, a matched-random-tilt
\emph{placebo}, a RavenPack-sentiment ablation, and---decisively---two LLMs on the \emph{same}
headlines: \textbf{qwen2.5-14B} (released 2024, training \emph{overlaps} the window) and \textbf{Llama~2}
(cutoff Sept.\ 2022, \emph{before} it). The language effect is measured at the exact optimum to separate
it from optimizer noise.

\section{Results and Contribution}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{compare_clean_vs_contaminated.pdf}
\caption{The out-of-sample premium of LLM-conditioned tilts over matched random tilts collapses from
$+0.76\%$ ($p\!\approx\!0$) with a training-overlapping LLM to $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$) with a pre-cutoff LLM
on the same headlines (left); per-quarter effects over four decision dates (right).}
\label{fig:main}
\end{figure}
Over 1{,}600 instances (Dow~30, $n{=}14$ qubits, hold~5, four quarterly dates 2023-12--2024-09), the
training-overlapping LLM beats matched random tilts by $+0.76\%$/quarter ($p\!\approx\!0$, positive in
3/4 quarters); the pre-cutoff LLM, on identical headlines, yields $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, 2/4)---%
indistinguishable from random (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}): the signal does not survive removing the model's
hindsight. The language does have leverage (a $\pm10\%$ view changes the exact optimum in $47$--$68\%$ of
instances), yet shallow QAOA recovers the true optimum only $7.7\%$ of the time; we make no
quantum-advantage claim and run the economic test at the exact optimum because the solver is too noisy.
Limitations: four decision dates (instance-level $p$ is pseudo-replicated), small-$n$ exact simulation,
large-cap US, no transaction costs---none inflating the central result. \textbf{We contribute} a
transparent, bias-controlled pipeline linking language to a quantum portfolio optimizer and a quantified
warning: LLM training-horizon look-ahead can manufacture out-of-sample ``alpha'' that vanishes with a
pre-cutoff model---LLM-for-finance studies should date their model against their backtest. All results
are reproducible from released code with fixed seeds.
