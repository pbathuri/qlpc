\begin{abstract}
We introduce \emph{language-conditioned} QAOA: a pipeline in which a large language model (LLM) reads
financial news and emits per-asset convictions that tilt the cost Hamiltonian of a cardinality-constrained
Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) portfolio optimizer. We then ask, with strong bias
controls, whether the language adds out-of-sample value.
It appears to: an LLM released during the evaluation window (qwen2.5-14B, Sept.\ 2024) beats
matched random tilts by $+0.76\%$/quarter ($p\!\approx\!0$). But a \emph{genuinely out-of-sample} LLM
(Llama~2, cutoff Sept.\ 2022) on the \emph{same} headlines yields $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, indistinguishable
from random): the benefit appears only for the model released into the test period---consistent
with \emph{training-horizon hindsight} rather than prediction (the pre-cutoff model is also smaller, a
caveat we note). We contribute the language$\to$quantum conditioning pipeline, a reusable bias-control
template, and a quantified training-horizon look-ahead warning for LLM-for-finance evaluation.
\keywords{QAOA \and Quantum optimization \and LLM-conditioning \and Look-ahead bias \and Quantum finance.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Pipeline and Method}
Two lines straddle a gap: Lee et al.~\cite{lee2025llmbl} drive Black--Litterman views
with an LLM but optimize \emph{classically}, while Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023qbl} bring numerically
predicted views into a \emph{quantum} pipeline with no natural language. We occupy the middle---an LLM's
language conditions the quantum optimizer---and treat ``do news views help?'' as a
\emph{measurement problem}. Holding $B$ of $n$ assets to maximize the mean--variance objective
$\mu^\top x - q\,x^\top\Sigma x$ over a fixed Dow-30 universe (current, not point-in-time, constituents;
CRSP returns; shrinkage covariance), we map this binary selection to a diagonal Ising Hamiltonian solved
by QAOA~\cite{farhi2014qaoa}. A local LLM reads RavenPack news headlines time-stamped \emph{strictly before}
each quarterly decision date into convictions $v_i\in[-1,1]$; a $\pm10\%$ view tilts $\mu\to\mu+s\,v$ (the
standard sentiment-to-view convention), estimated on the train window and evaluated on the held-out next
quarter. Bias controls: hundreds of (subset $\times$ window) instances from a fixed
seed, pre-registered metrics, a matched-random-tilt \emph{placebo}, an off-the-shelf-sentiment (RavenPack
ESS) ablation, and---decisively---two LLMs on the \emph{same} headlines: \textbf{qwen2.5-14B} (released
Sept.\ 2024, training plausibly \emph{spans} the window) and \textbf{Llama~2} (documented cutoff Sept.\ 2022,
\emph{before} it).
The language effect is read at the exact optimum, free of optimizer noise.

\section{Results}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.34\textwidth]{compare_premium.pdf}
\caption{Out-of-sample premium over matched random tilts: a training-overlapping LLM (qwen2.5-14B)
vs.\ a pre-cutoff LLM (Llama~2) on identical headlines.}
\label{fig:main}
\end{figure}
Over 1{,}600 instances (Dow~30, $n{=}14$ qubits, hold~5, four quarterly dates 2023-12--2024-09), the
training-overlapping LLM beats matched random tilts by a language-specific premium of $+0.76\%$/quarter
on changed-optimum instances (instance-level $p\!\approx\!0$, positive in 3/4 quarters); the pre-cutoff
LLM, on identical headlines, yields $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, 2/4)---indistinguishable from random
(Fig.~\ref{fig:main}). The conditioning has real
mechanism leverage (a $\pm10\%$ view changes the
exact optimum in $47$--$68\%$ of instances), yet QAOA at depth $p{=}4$ recovers the true optimum only
$7.7\%$ of the time; we make no quantum-advantage claim. Limitations: four decision dates (instance-level
$p$ is pseudo-replicated); the clean and contaminated LLMs differ in capability as well as cutoff, so the
two are not fully separated; small-$n$ simulation; fixed-membership large-cap US; no costs. None overturn the takeaway: an
LLM whose training overlaps the backtest can manufacture out-of-sample ``alpha'' that a pre-cutoff model
does not---a look-ahead that LLM-for-finance studies must guard against by dating their model against the
evaluation window. Fixed seeds; code released upon publication.
