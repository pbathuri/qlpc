\title{Does Language Really Help a Quantum Portfolio Optimizer? A Bias-Controlled Study and a
Training-Horizon Confound in LLM-Conditioned QAOA}
\titlerunning{A Training-Horizon Confound in LLM-Conditioned QAOA}
\author{Pradyot Bathuri\inst{1}}
\authorrunning{P. Bathuri}
\institute{Indiana University, Bloomington, USA \\ \email{pbathuri@iu.edu}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to turn financial text into views that drive
allocation, and quantum optimizers are proposed for the discrete selection these allocations require. We
build a bias-controlled pipeline that bridges the two---an LLM reads point-in-time news into per-asset
convictions that tilt the cost Hamiltonian of a cardinality-constrained Quantum Approximate Optimization
Algorithm (QAOA) portfolio optimizer---and ask whether the language adds genuine out-of-sample value. It
appears to: an LLM whose training overlaps the evaluation window beats matched random tilts by $+0.76\%$
per quarter ($p\!\approx\!0$). But with a \emph{genuinely out-of-sample} LLM (Llama~2, cutoff Sept.\ 2022)
on the \emph{same} headlines, the premium collapses to $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$, indistinguishable from
random): the apparent signal is the model's \emph{training-horizon hindsight}, not prediction. We
contribute the reproducible methodology and a concrete warning about a subtle, pervasive confound in
LLM-for-finance backtests.
\keywords{QAOA \and Quantum optimization \and LLM-conditioning \and Look-ahead bias \and Quantum finance.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Two recent lines sit either side of a gap: Lee et al.~\cite{lee2025llmbl} drive Black--Litterman views
with an LLM but optimize \emph{classically}, while Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023qbl} bring numerically
predicted views into a \emph{quantum} pipeline with no natural language. We occupy the middle---an LLM
reads real news into a tilt on a QAOA~\cite{farhi2014qaoa} cost Hamiltonian---and treat ``do LLM news
views help?'' as a \emph{measurement problem}: is a positive backtest signal, or an artifact?

\section{Method}
The universe is an index (Dow~30), not author-chosen; returns are CRSP total returns with Ledoit--Wolf
covariance~\cite{ledoit2004}. Holding $B$ of $n$ assets to maximize $\mu^\top x - q\,x^\top\Sigma x$
\cite{markowitz1952}, we map the penalized binary problem to a diagonal Ising Hamiltonian solved by QAOA.
An LLM reads RavenPack headlines time-stamped \emph{strictly before} each quarterly decision date into
convictions $v_i\in[-1,1]$; a $\pm10\%$ annual view tilts $\mu\to\mu+s\,v$ (the sentiment-to-view
convention~\cite{black1992litterman}). Estimation uses the train window only; evaluation uses the
held-out next quarter. Bias controls---the point of the paper---are: hundreds of (subset $\times$
rolling-window) instances from a fixed seed; pre-registered metrics; a \emph{placebo} of matched random
tilts (does the \emph{language} matter, or any tilt?); an ablation against RavenPack's own sentiment
score; and, decisively, two LLMs on the \emph{same} headlines---\textbf{qwen2.5-14B} (released 2024,
training \emph{overlaps} the evaluation window) and \textbf{Llama~2} (cutoff Sept.\ 2022, \emph{before}
it). The language effect is measured at the exact optimum to separate it from optimizer noise.

\section{Results}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{compare_clean_vs_contaminated.pdf}
\caption{The out-of-sample premium of LLM-conditioned tilts over matched random tilts collapses from
$+0.76\%$ ($p\!\approx\!0$) with a training-overlapping LLM to $-0.23\%$ ($p=0.94$) with a pre-cutoff LLM
on the same headlines (left); per-quarter effects, over only four decision dates (right).}
\label{fig:main}
\end{figure}
Over 1{,}600 instances (Dow~30, $n{=}14$ qubits, hold~5, four quarterly dates 2023-12--2024-09), the
training-overlapping LLM beats matched random tilts by a language-specific premium of $+0.76\%$/quarter
($p\!\approx\!0$, positive in 3/4 quarters). The pre-cutoff LLM, on identical headlines, yields $-0.23\%$
($p=0.94$, 2/4): statistically indistinguishable from random (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}). The signal does not
survive removal of the model's hindsight. The language does have mechanism leverage---a $\pm10\%$ view
changes the exact optimal portfolio in $47$--$68\%$ of instances---yet shallow QAOA is a \emph{weak}
exact optimizer, recovering the true optimum in only $7.7\%$ of instances. We make no quantum-advantage
claim; QAOA is the studied object, and the economic test is run at the exact optimum because the solver
is too noisy to rely on. \emph{Scope:} four decision dates (instance-level $p$ is pseudo-replicated; the
honest unit is the quarter), small-$n$ exact simulation (no hardware), large-cap US only, no transaction
costs---none of which inflate the central result: the premium's disappearance under a clean LLM.

\section{Contribution}
We provide (i)~a transparent, bias-controlled pipeline linking natural language to a quantum portfolio
optimizer, and (ii)~a quantified warning---LLM training-horizon look-ahead can manufacture out-of-sample
``alpha'' that vanishes with a pre-cutoff model. LLM-for-finance studies should date their model against
their backtest. All results are reproducible from released code with fixed seeds and committed data
caches, and the instance sweep scales on HPC for the many-period study that significance ultimately needs.
